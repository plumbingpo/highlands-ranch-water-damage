Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Highlands Ranch

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. For Highlands Ranch homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. Getting it out is the work we do. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Keep Power AwayIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Have the Area MeteredAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Read your policy and talk to your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Access to the pipe, with a sink cabinet being cheaper to work in than a slab or a closed wall.
  • How long the pipe leaked and how far the moisture spread from it.
  • How much drywall, insulation, cabinetry or flooring has to be removed and replaced.
  • How many rooms or levels the water reached, including a ceiling below the leak.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • A compression fitting or supply line connection at a sink or toilet that has loosened or worn with use.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Decayed Wall Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Hidden Mold Growth

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Our Process

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan walls, floors and cabinets with moisture meters and a thermal camera to mark every wet area, including spots well away from the pipe where water has traveled along framing.

2

Open What Is Needed

To get at wet cavities we open only specific spots, take out unsalvageable cabinet parts, drywall or insulation, and seal anything moldy in bags so its spores stay out of the rest of the home.

3

Structural Drying

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Treat and Document

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and you receive photos along with the moisture logs. Those records show the area was dry before repairs and help with any claim.
