Wet Carpet Drying: What Highlands Ranch Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. For Highlands Ranch homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • A clammy, cool patch underfoot that lingers well past the original spill
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Water Held in the Pad

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Swollen Subfloor

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Mold on the Backing

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.
